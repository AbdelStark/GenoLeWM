\begin{abstract}
GenoLeWM is an action-conditioned latent-prediction pipeline that freezes \carbon and trains a cross-attention predictor over explicit genomic edits. This paper originally interpreted the released \texttt{v0.2.1-r1} benchmark as a negative scientific result. A post-release audit invalidates that interpretation. Although the release configuration set \texttt{normalize: true}, the encoder returned raw pooled source and target states, while the predictor always returned a unit-norm vector. Training also used incompatible pooling coordinates: reference states fell back to global-mean pooling because no edit locus was supplied, whereas targets were pooled around the edit; rollout source and target states were edit-centered, and historical distractors could be centered elsewhere. Moreover, every historical ``centered'' pool omitted the leading \texttt{<dna>} control-token offset and was shifted one hidden token left. The pinned Carbon tokenizer code also made an unpinned, network-capable transitive load of \texttt{Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Base}, so its local hash did not define a self-contained runtime. On the released rollout slices, mean source/target norms were $33.982/33.595$ (phased) and $29.253/29.089$ (synthetic), whereas mean prediction norm was $1.000$ on both. Consequently, released $L_2$ losses, surprise scores, $L_2$ rollout metrics, and conclusions derived from them do not evaluate the intended normalized, coordinate-matched method. The released cosine values ($0.289$ and $0.302$, versus source-state values $0.998$ and $0.991$) remain reproducible historical implementation measurements because cosine is scale-invariant, but they are confounded by invalid mixed-scale, mixed-pooling training and a train/rollout distribution mismatch; they do not establish model capability or a failure mechanism. The actual released run used seed $271828$, $10{,}000$ optimizer steps and $80{,}000$ samples. It was labeled \texttt{phase2}, but used no LoRA, and its target-only Gaussian KL term was constant with respect to trainable parameters and supplied no gradient. We preserve the released numbers for auditability, withdraw the mechanistic attribution and capability conclusions, and require a corrected end-to-end run under a new content-addressed identity before making either positive or negative scientific claims.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Post-Release Validity Correction}
\textbf{Correction issued 10 July 2026.} This version supersedes the original interpretation of all \texttt{v0.2.1-r1} model-quality results. The release receipts establish included-artifact identity, not semantic or transitive-runtime validity, and therefore did not detect that \texttt{normalize: true} was ignored, that pooling omitted the leading DNA control-token offset, or that the pinned tokenizer code could fetch an unpinned Qwen tokenizer. Four boundaries now govern the paper: (i)~all reported values remain available only as historical measurements of the released implementation; (ii)~all $L_2$-dependent results, including surprise-based VEP, are invalid as evaluations of the intended method; (iii)~cosine measurements are retained but cannot support a capability or causal conclusion because training used mismatched scale and pooling coordinates while rollout evaluation used a different distribution; and (iv)~no corrected result may reuse the \texttt{v0.2.1-r1} identity. A new checkpoint, dataset/evaluation manifest, metric report, and receipt graph are required after normalization, token-layout, pooling-coordinate, and self-contained-runtime contracts are fixed and tested end to end.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

DNA foundation models~\citep{dnabert2021,hyenadna2023,nucleotidetransformer2025,caduceus2024,evo2024} answer a representational question: \emph{what is the embedding, or the likelihood, of this sequence?} A different and largely unanswered question is \emph{interventional}: given a reference genomic window and an explicit edit (a single-nucleotide variant, an insertion, or a deletion), can a model predict the \emph{consequence} of that edit directly in representation space, cheaply enough to score variants, roll out multi-edit haplotypes, and plan edit sets? An affirmative answer would turn a static genomic encoder into an action-conditioned \emph{world model}~\citep{worldmodels2018,dreamerv3} whose actions are discrete genomic edits.

\method tests this question in its most economical form. The intended method freezes a pretrained DNA encoder, \carbon~\citep{carbon500m}, and trains only a small predictor: a reference window $\wref$ is encoded at the edit locus to a state $\st=\enc(\wref)\in\Real^{1024}$, an edit action $a$ is embedded by a lightweight action encoder, and a cross-attention predictor estimates the post-edit state $\shat=\gpred(\st,a)$, supervised against a coordinate-matched frozen encoding $\stp=\enc(\walt)$ of the edited window (\cref{fig:architecture}). The implementation also exposes surprise scoring, autoregressive rollout, and CEM planning. The released experiment, however, did not instantiate the intended state contract: encoder states were raw, training source and target pooling differed, and predictor outputs were unit-normalized. We therefore describe the architecture but do not treat \texttt{v0.2.1-r1} as a valid test of it.

\paragraph{The headline is a validity correction.} The released benchmark cannot determine whether \method beats, matches, or trails its intended baselines. Historical VEP deltas include $-0.1875$ (ClinVar coding AUROC) and $-0.3125$ (ClinVar non-coding AUROC), while historical rollout cosine is ${\approx}\,0.29$ versus ${\approx}\,0.99$ for the source-state baseline. Those values describe the flawed implementation, not the normalized-state method. In particular, the VEP score and rollout $L_2$ metric compare unit-norm predictions with targets whose mean norms are about $29$--$34$. The content-addressed package makes this failure reproducible, but its receipts bind identities and bytes; they do not validate the semantics of the computation.

\paragraph{Contributions after correction.} This paper now makes four narrower contributions.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{An implemented research architecture} (\cref{sec:method}): a discrete-edit action encoder, cross-attention predictor, rollout wrapper, surprise interface, and CEM planner. No behavioral capability is inferred from the released run.
\item \textbf{A content-addressed train$\to$eval$\to$benchmark$\to$replay pipeline} (\cref{sec:systems}) whose strengths and limits are now explicit: it binds artifacts and outputs, but does not establish semantic correctness.
\item \textbf{A post-release validity audit} (\cref{sec:experiments,sec:discussion}) that preserves the historical measurements, reports the state-norm and token-layout mismatches, discloses the unpinned transitive tokenizer, corrects the run metadata, and identifies which metrics and conclusions must be withdrawn.
\item \textbf{A falsifiable correction protocol} (\cref{sec:future}) requiring self-contained tokenization, token-layout-derived centers, consistent state normalization, gradient-bearing regularization if claimed, distribution-matched evaluation, and a new content-addressed run identity before scientific interpretation resumes.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What we do \emph{not} claim.} \method is not a clinical, diagnostic, or decision-support tool. We make no model-quality, world-model-capability, surprise-validity, planning-usefulness, or mechanistic claim from \texttt{v0.2.1-r1}; we also make no privacy, runtime-execution, or efficiency guarantee. The released VEP, rollout $L_2$, and planning-distance results are not valid evaluations of the intended normalized-state method. The historical cosine results are insufficient for interpretation rather than evidence for a specific failure mode. We collect these boundaries in \cref{sec:limitations}.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\method sits at the intersection of four research programs. DNA foundation models supply the encoder; variant-effect prediction supplies the evaluation and the foil; joint-embedding predictive architectures supply the intended objective; and latent world models supply the action-conditioned framing and planning interface. The released experiment does not support a scientific comparison to these programs because it violated its state contract.

\paragraph{DNA foundation models and genomic functional prediction.}
Masked, causal, and state-space sequence models include DNABERT~\citep{dnabert2021}, the multi-species Nucleotide Transformer~\citep{nucleotidetransformer2025}, HyenaDNA~\citep{hyenadna2023}, Caduceus~\citep{caduceus2024}, and the genome-scale generative models Evo and Evo~2~\citep{evo2024,evo2_2025}; they expose sequence representations or likelihoods. AlphaGenome~\citep{alphagenome2025} instead predicts functional genomic tracks and can compare reference and alternate sequences. \carbon~\citep{carbon500m} is the frozen sequence encoder used here. The intended \method distinction is narrower: a structured edit action conditions a learned latent transition operator. The released experiment does not establish that this operator is valid.

\paragraph{Variant-effect prediction and its resources, and the precise sense in which we are not a clinical predictor.}
The released evaluation used ClinVar clinical interpretations~\citep{clinvar2014}, gnomAD population frequencies~\citep{gnomad2020}, TraitGym~\citep{traitgym2025}, a BRCA2 saturation genome-editing functional assay, and a \carbon zero-shot baseline; AlphaMissense~\citep{alphamissense2023} provides broader field context. \method is not a clinical predictor. No pathogenicity label enters predictor training, and no output should be treated as a decision. More importantly after this correction, the released surprise score was an $L_2$ residual between unit-norm predictions and unnormalized target states. Its ranking metrics therefore cannot answer even the narrow internal question of whether action conditioning adds signal over \carbon. We retain the benchmark rows as provenance records, not clinical or comparative evidence.

\paragraph{JEPAs and anti-collapse SSL: intended objective versus released computation.}
The objective lineage is the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture. I-JEPA~\citep{jepa2023} predicts in representation space, and LeJEPA~\citep{lejepa2025} motivates distribution regularization. \method intended to combine cosine and normalized-state $L_2$ prediction terms and, in Phase~2, a Gaussian regularizer with encoder adaptation. The released run did something materially different: its configuration said \texttt{phase2}, but \carbon remained frozen with no LoRA; the KL was computed only from frozen target states, making it constant with respect to trainable parameters; and the $L_2$ term compared unit-norm predictions with raw target states. Thus neither the intended normalized prediction objective nor a gradient-bearing Phase-2 regularizer was evaluated. Any proposed latent-residual or representation-geometry explanation remains a hypothesis for a corrected experiment, not a finding of this release.

\paragraph{Latent world models and planning: a world model whose action is a discrete genomic edit.}
The architectural lineage runs from Ha and Schmidhuber's World Models~\citep{worldmodels2018} through the Dreamer family~\citep{dreamerv3} to sampling-based latent model-predictive control. These systems may support continuous or discrete control actions; \method's distinction is the structured genomic semantics $(\text{position},\text{type},\text{ref},\text{alt})$ and a factored CEM search space. That interface is an architectural research scaffold. Because both training and latent-distance evaluation were affected by the state-contract violation, the released planning trace demonstrates code-path execution only; it does not show a valid transition model or planning capability.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\method has three trained-or-frozen components (a frozen state encoder, a trainable action encoder, and a trainable predictor) and three downstream operations: surprise scoring, rollout, and planning. We distinguish the intended normalized-state method from the released \texttt{v0.2.1-r1} computation. This distinction is load-bearing: configuration intent is not evidence of runtime behavior.

\subsection{State encoder (frozen)}
The encoder is \carbon, a $500$M-parameter decoder-only DNA language model (LLaMA-style, hidden size $1024$), loaded at a pinned revision in \texttt{bf16} and frozen in the released run. The code default is a $12{,}288$\,bp window, but the released \texttt{v0.2.1} placed-window manifest supplied $4{,}096$\,bp windows (padded to the tokenizer's six-base multiple). The intended representation pools layer-$20$ hidden states by a centered mean over a $\pm 8$-token window ($\pm 48$\,bp) around one shared edit locus. Carbon emits one leading \texttt{<dna>} control token, so let
\begin{equation}
u_t \;=\; \mathrm{mean}\!\Big(H_L\big[c-r:\,c+r+1\big]\Big),\qquad
\st^{\mathrm{intended}}=\frac{u_t}{\lVert u_t\rVert_2},\qquad c=1+\lfloor \text{locus}/6\rfloor,\; r=8.
\end{equation}
The configuration set \texttt{normalize: true}, but the released encoder returned raw pooled vectors without applying the second operation. Worse, training called the source encoder without an edit locus, which triggered global-mean fallback, while targets used a historical ``centered'' operator with $c_{\mathrm{hist}}=\lfloor\text{locus}/6\rfloor$. It omitted the leading control token, shifting every intended center one hidden token left and sometimes centering the pool on \texttt{<dna>}. Rollout generation used the same shifted rule for source and target states at the first target edit, but centered historical distractors at each distractor's own first edit. Thus the release mixed scale, coordinate frame, and token layout. The reported norm audit concerns these shifted rollout rows: $33.982/33.595$ for source/target phased states and $29.253/29.089$ for source/target synthetic states. These were not benign metadata discrepancies: they changed optimization, retrieval geometry, and every $L_2$-based score. Cache v1 also omitted the pooling center from its key, so centered states for the same sequence could collide. The $\pm 48$\,bp radius is additionally narrower than the RFC's $\pm1536$\,bp default.

The pinned Carbon \texttt{tokenizer.py} created a separate reproducibility failure: it called \texttt{Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Base} without a revision or local-only constraint. Pinning and hashing the local Carbon custom-tokenizer file therefore did not bind the transitive tokenizer implementation and did not make the runtime self-contained. The corrected source path includes its local pure-DNA tokenizer implementation in encoder identity, validates the control-token/six-mer layout, and derives $c$ from the observed DNA-content start. These are code-contract repairs only; no corrected checkpoint or model-quality result follows from them.

\subsection{Action encoder (trainable)}
An edit is represented as an \texttt{EditSpec} (absolute VCF coordinates $\text{chrom},\text{pos},\text{ref},\text{alt}$) or its window-relative form \texttt{RelEdit}, with edit type derived from the $(\text{ref},\text{alt})$ lengths into $\{$SNV, INS, DEL, MNV, INDEL$\}$ and a $16$\,bp cap on either allele. The action encoder maps an edit to $\aemb\in\Real^{64}$ through four sub-encoders, namely a base-pair-resolution sinusoidal position embedding ($\Real^{128}$), a learned type embedding ($\Real^{64}$), and a \emph{shared} small Transformer (``SeqMicroEncoder'') applied to the reference and alternate alleles ($\Real^{256}$ each); these are concatenated to $\Real^{704}$ and projected by a two-layer MLP with GELU and LayerNorm. The sinusoidal schedule uses the window length as its frequency base rather than the conventional $10{,}000$.

\subsection{Predictor (trainable, residual)}
The predictor $\gpred$ is a cross-attention Transformer over $[\st\,;a_1\,;\dots;a_K]$, with six cross-attention blocks, two self-attention blocks, hidden dimension $768$, eight heads, GELU, pre-LayerNorm, learned token-type and step-position embeddings, and a causal mask. Its residual read-out is always $L_2$-normalized, with the final MLP layer zero-initialized:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:residual}
\shat \;=\; \frac{\st + \mathrm{MLP}\!\big(\gpred(\st,\aemb)\big)}{\big\lVert \st + \mathrm{MLP}\!\big(\gpred(\st,\aemb)\big)\big\rVert_2}.
\end{equation}
Under the intended unit-norm contract, zero initialization would make $\shat=\st$. Under the released contract it instead made $\shat=u_t/\lVert u_t\rVert_2$, while the reported source-state baseline remained the raw $u_t$. Thus the previous claim that initialization exactly equaled the evaluated ``predict-no-change'' baseline was false for this run. An autoregressive wrapper (\texttt{ARPredictor}) unrolls the predictor with KV-caching and upcasts the output MLP to \texttt{fp32} beyond horizon $20$.

\subsection{Released training objective (\texttt{phase2} label, frozen encoder)}
The prediction term was
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:loss}
\Lpred(\shat,\stp) \;=\; \alpha\,\big(1-\cos(\shat,\stp)\big)\;+\;\beta\,\frac{\lVert \shat-\stp\rVert_2^2}{d},\qquad \alpha{=}1.0,\;\beta{=}0.1,\;d{=}1024,
\end{equation}
averaged uniformly over rollout steps with a mask. Because $\shat$ had norm $1$ and $\stp$ had norm about $29$--$34$, the $L_2$ term did not measure the intended unit-sphere residual. The run was configured as \texttt{phase2}, so the implementation added $\gamma\Lreg$ with $\gamma{=}0.5$, where $\Lreg=\tfrac12(\lVert\mu\rVert^2+\mathrm{tr}\,\Sigma-\log\det(\Sigma+\epsilon I)-d)$. However, $\Lreg$ was computed only from frozen target states. No LoRA parameters existed, and the term was constant with respect to the predictor and action encoder; it supplied no optimization gradient. The released run used AdamW, seed $271828$, $10{,}000$ optimizer steps, $80{,}000$ samples, and deterministic mode. The previously reported seed $104729$ and $20{,}000$ steps describe a different first-experiment lineage, not \texttt{v0.2.1-r1}.

\subsection{Surprise scoring}
The intended raw surprise is the normalized-state residual $\sigma_{\mathrm{raw}}(v)=\lVert \gpred(\st,a_v)-\enc(\mathrm{apply}(v,\wref))\rVert_2$, calibrated by an empirical context-bucket CDF. In the release, the first operand was unit norm and the second was raw. Calibration can rank a flawed quantity consistently but cannot restore its intended meaning. Accordingly, all released surprise-based VEP metrics are historical implementation measurements and are withdrawn as evidence about variant effects or action conditioning.

\subsection{Latent planning}
Given an initial state and a target latent, the CEM planner samples discrete edit sequences, scores predictor rollouts by latent distance, and refits a factored proposal. It uses defaults of horizon $5$, five iterations, $1024$ samples, and $64$ elites. The search path executes without further encoder calls after setup, but its released distances inherited the incompatible state scales. The trace is therefore integration evidence only, not evidence that the planner optimized a scientifically meaningful objective.

\subsection{The efficiency thesis and its honest scope}
\label{sec:method-efficiency}
The design rationale is amortization: pay one \carbon forward pass per reference window, then issue latent queries through the smaller predictor. Rollout and planning make no further encoder calls after setup, whereas single-variant surprise scoring requires an edited-window encode. These are call-graph properties, not demonstrated efficiency or utility. The released latency benchmark exercises a cold subprocess and the model-quality path is invalid, so no end-to-end efficiency--quality claim follows.

\section{Systems and Reproducibility}
\label{sec:systems}

The most defensible positive contribution of this work is infrastructural but narrower than originally stated: the released artifacts and numbers are content-addressed and replayable. The validity correction demonstrates what this does \emph{not} provide. Reproducible execution can faithfully reproduce a semantically invalid computation, so provenance must be paired with tested scientific contracts~\citep{reproducibility2021}.

\paragraph{Content-addressed provenance.}
A canonical-JSON serialization (sorted keys, rejected NaN/Inf, UTF-8) feeds a SHA-256 scheme in which the model identity hashes the model manifest and its artifact files. Each scoring call emits a checksum receipt binding the model id, input commitment, output, calibration hash, and runtime metadata. These receipts certify byte-level integrity and identity for included files, not attestation or scientific validity. In particular, the input commitment recorded \texttt{normalize: true} while runtime behavior ignored it. Historical encoder identity also omitted the runtime package, and even hashing the later local \texttt{tokenizer.py} would not have bound its unpinned transitive Qwen load. The receipt therefore committed to included bytes and configuration intent without proving the state transformation or a self-contained runtime.

\paragraph{Evidence-bound benchmark suite.}
The benchmark suite executes a fixed step graph and records the path, SHA-256, and size of each output. The release reported $28$ verified outputs and all required benchmark rows. That proves coverage of the declared graph, not correctness of the state representation or metric operands. The normalization contract was absent from the semantic gate.

\paragraph{Why the original verifier was insufficient.}
The paper toolchain re-renders tables from artifacts and rejects stale text, placeholders, and mutated evidence. It also required selected negative deltas to be present. Those checks prevented transcription drift but mistook sign constraints for validity constraints: they never checked source, target, and prediction norms; whether \texttt{normalize: true} affected runtime states; whether base-pair loci mapped after the DNA control token; whether tokenizer dependencies were local and pinned; whether a claimed regularizer had a gradient path; or whether train and rollout distributions matched. A corrected release gate must establish those invariants before accepting any metric sign, and the corrected run must receive a new identity rather than overwriting this historical graph.

\paragraph{On-device posture.}
The current source runtime enforces a fail-closed network guard during inference and uses a self-contained pure-DNA tokenizer; the released lineage did not satisfy that tokenizer boundary because its custom code could perform the unpinned Qwen lookup during setup. The project also ships local-first importers for consumer personal-genome formats, with a redaction filter that drops DNA-like strings and a deny-list of personal fields at the logging boundary. We describe these as current engineering choices for local execution; we explicitly do \emph{not} present them as historical runtime proof or a privacy guarantee.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Setup.}
The historical measurements come from one released identity (\cref{tab:artifacts}): model \texttt{geno-lewm-v0.2.1-r1}, dataset snapshot \texttt{geno-lewm-data-v0.2.1-r1}, commit \texttt{d9b06815}, evaluated on one NVIDIA~H200. The checkpoint used seed $271828$, $10{,}000$ optimizer steps, and $80{,}000$ samples. Its configuration said \texttt{phase2}, but \carbon was frozen, no LoRA was present, and the target-only KL supplied no gradient. The historical baselines were \carbon zero-shot for VEP and an unchanged raw source state for rollout. We report the rows to document the invalidated release, not to compare scientific methods.

\subsection{Variant-effect prediction}
\label{sec:exp-vep}
\Cref{tab:vep} and \cref{fig:vep} preserve the historical VEP rows. The GenoLeWM score was derived from the invalid $L_2$ residual and therefore is not a measurement of the intended surprise function. The historical values include ClinVar coding AUROC $0.734$ ($\Delta{=}-0.1875$), ClinVar non-coding AUROC $0.563$ ($\Delta{=}-0.3125$), BRCA2 Spearman $0.149$ ($\Delta{=}-0.328$), and TraitGym Spearman $-0.028$ ($\Delta{=}+0.056$). These numbers remain useful for tracing the released artifact but support neither superiority nor inferiority. Independent of the normalization bug, the slices are also small ($N{=}16$ for each ClinVar slice, $N{=}32$ for the BRCA2 continuous-score slice, and $N{=}32$ for the TraitGym binary-label slice), so a corrected rerun must be powered before interpretation.

\subsection{Latent rollout fidelity}
\label{sec:exp-rollout}
The rollout track (\cref{tab:rollout}, \cref{fig:rollout}) exposes the state-contract violation. Mean source/target/prediction norms are $33.982/33.595/1.000$ for phased haplotypes and $29.253/29.089/1.000$ for synthetic edit chains. Hence the historical $L_2$ values and deltas are invalid comparisons. \section{Discussion: Why the Released Result Is Not Interpretable}
\label{sec:discussion}

The original paper attributed the observed metrics to a structural latent-residual trap. That attribution is withdrawn. The current evidence establishes a contract violation and several confounders, not a mechanism.

\paragraph{Normalization contract violation (established).}
The encoder accepted \texttt{normalize: true} but emitted raw pooled states, whereas the predictor explicitly normalized its output. The roughly $29$--$34\times$ norm gap makes the released $L_2$ loss and metric dominated by operand scale. It also invalidates the previous identity-at-initialization equivalence, because normalizing the raw source is not equal to returning the raw source baseline.

\paragraph{Pooling-coordinate contract violation (established).}
Training source states used global-mean fallback, targets used nominally edit-centered pooling, and rollout evaluation supplied nominally edit-centered sources. Every nominal center omitted the leading \texttt{<dna>} token and was therefore one hidden token too far left; historical candidate states could also use different loci, while cache v1 did not commit a center token. This makes the latent operands coordinate-incompatible independently of normalization and sharpens the train/rollout mismatch; no metric from this lineage isolates a learned transition.

\paragraph{Transitive runtime-identity violation (established).}
The pinned Carbon custom tokenizer source performed an unpinned, network-capable \texttt{Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Base} load. A hash of that source file identifies the call site, not the mutable transitive tokenizer it resolves. Claims that the historical runtime was fully local or that the custom-tokenizer hash alone completed its identity are withdrawn. The corrected identity includes the local pure-DNA tokenizer implementation, but that code-path repair supplies no behavioral evidence.

\paragraph{Regularization mismatch (established).}
The \texttt{phase2} label did not imply encoder adaptation. With no LoRA and a KL computed only on frozen targets, the regularizer had zero gradient with respect to all trainable parameters. The run therefore cannot test claims about Phase-2 joint representation learning or anti-collapse behavior.

\paragraph{Cosine and distribution shift (unresolved).}
Cosine removes scale at evaluation time, so the historical cosine values are not arithmetically invalid for that reason alone. They are nevertheless downstream of training with the mixed-scale $L_2$ term, and the rollout settings do not match the training distribution. Without a corrected, distribution-matched evaluation, the cosine gap cannot identify whether the cause is normalization, optimization, distribution shift, action-conditioning failure, or encoder geometry.

\paragraph{Mechanistic attribution withdrawn.}
The latent-residual trap, edit-agnostic encoder geometry, and identity attraction remain plausible hypotheses, but this release does not distinguish them. We therefore withdraw the claims that freezing caused the observed gap, that the model learned a distorted copy, or that edit-conditioned prediction is unlearnable in this regime. Those questions reopen only after the corrected control run.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The primary limitation is a validity failure, not merely low power. (i)~\emph{State contract}: \texttt{normalize: true} was ignored for source and target states, while predictions were unit norm; all $L_2$-dependent results are invalid for the intended method. (ii)~\emph{Pooling coordinates}: training sources were global, targets and rollout sources used a one-token-left ``center,'' historical candidates could use different loci, and cache v1 omitted that identity field. (iii)~\emph{Runtime identity}: the pinned custom tokenizer performed an unpinned, network-capable transitive Qwen load. (iv)~\emph{Training semantics}: the run was labeled \texttt{phase2} but used no LoRA, and its target-only KL supplied no gradient. (v)~\emph{Distribution shift}: historical rollout cosine is confounded by mismatch between training and rollout distributions. (vi)~\emph{Statistical power}: ClinVar slices are $N{=}16$ and rollout slices are $N{=}8$. (vii)~\emph{Coverage and configuration drift}: training and holdout coverage are narrow, while pooling radius and predictor depth diverge from design RFCs. (viii)~\emph{Efficiency and planning}: warm-cache latency was not measured, the AR speed test used synthetic tensors and a benchmark-specific predictor shape, and planning distances are invalid under the state mismatch. (ix)~\emph{Receipts}: checksums certify included identity, not transitive dependencies, execution semantics, scientific validity, or privacy. These limitations preclude model-quality and mechanistic conclusions from this release.

\section{Historical Released Measurements}
\label{sec:negative}

For auditability, we preserve the released rows without labeling them scientific findings. Historical VEP deltas include ClinVar non-coding AUROC $-0.3125$, BRCA2 Spearman $-0.328$, and ClinVar coding AUROC $-0.1875$; they derive from an invalid $L_2$ surprise score. Historical rollout cosine deltas are $-0.709$ and $-0.690$; they are scale-invariant but confounded. Historical rollout $L_2$ deltas are $+31.19$ and $+25.64$; they are invalid because the operands had incompatible norms. The synthetic-tensor $K{=}20$ AR micro-benchmark measured $2.47\times$, the cold subprocess latency was about $115$\,s, and the planning trace stopped on patience at historical distance $23.66$. The correction changes the interpretation, not the archived bytes.

% ======================================================================
\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:reproducibility}

Every historical value is bound to the included identities in \cref{tab:artifacts}; the unpinned transitive tokenizer remains outside that graph. This enables forensic inspection of the flawed run, not exact self-contained reproduction of its runtime or reproduction of the intended method. The corrected experiment must not replace files beneath the existing identity or present a patched evaluator against the old checkpoint as a corrected result. It must issue a new model id, dataset/evaluation manifest, configuration hash, and receipt graph; resolve tokenization exclusively from committed local files; verify the DNA-content offset; record observed state norms at train and evaluation time; prove that \texttt{normalize: true} changes the tensors consumed by the predictor, loss, cache, scorer, rollout, and planner; and verify nonzero gradients for every regularizer described as active. Until that evidence exists, \texttt{v0.2.1-r1} remains an archived invalidated run.

% ======================================================================
\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

The first future experiment is a correction control, not a new modeling direction. It must (1) tokenize from a self-contained committed local package and validate the DNA control-token layout; (2) apply the same explicit normalization view to live and cached source and target states; (3) use the token-layout-derived shared pooling center for every compared state and commit that center in cache identity; (4) assert unit norms before loss, scoring, rollout, and planning; (5) train a true frozen-encoder baseline under \texttt{phase1}; (6) if Phase~2 is tested, add actual LoRA parameters and verify a nonzero KL gradient into them; (7) match train and rollout distributions or pre-register the shift and corresponding stratified analysis; (8) include shuffled-action and source-state controls; (9) use adequately powered evaluation slices and repeated seeds; and (10) publish under a new run identity. Only after this control may hypotheses such as residual-centric objectives, edit-direction probes, LoRA adaptation, alternative encoders, or scaling laws be evaluated. The old mechanistic program is retained as a set of hypotheses, not as consequences of \texttt{v0.2.1-r1}.

% ======================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We built and released an action-conditioned genomic latent-prediction pipeline, but the \texttt{v0.2.1-r1} experiment did not implement its declared state contract. Raw encoder states with norms near $29$--$34$ were compared against unit-norm predictions; source, target, and candidate pooling coordinates were inconsistent and nominal centers were one hidden token left; the tokenizer could resolve an unpinned network dependency; the run was labeled Phase~2 without LoRA; and its target-only KL had no gradient. We therefore withdraw the released $L_2$-based results, all VEP and planning interpretations derived from them, the mechanistic latent-residual attribution, historical self-contained-runtime claims, and all model-capability conclusions. Historical numbers remain attached to their included identities for auditability. The scientific question remains open and requires a corrected, self-contained, coordinate-matched, distribution-aware, adequately powered rerun under a new content-addressed identity.
